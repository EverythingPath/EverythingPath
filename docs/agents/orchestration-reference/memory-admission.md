# Memory admission (#759)

Reference for [Chaining heavy jobs](../orchestration/runbook.md#chaining-heavy-jobs): how `admission.sh`, `bounded.sh` and `capped.sh` reserve memory. Read it when a job waits longer than expected or a class looks wrong; the operator actions are in the runbook.

A model session reserves no memory; its heavy commands do. Memory means `real`: memory.current − file + shmem, without reclaimable cache. Every bounded unit and `$EP_ORCH/capped.sh` scope samples its own real memory every 5 s; a unit's real peak goes into its receipt (`realPeakBytes`), and every run gets one line in `$EP_ORCH/admission/peaks.log` when it ends, with two numbers: its real peak, the largest real value its sampler saw, and `memory.peak`, the kernel's peak with cache, never below real. A `capped.sh` command's `memory.peak` is read once more after the command and its sampler have ended, while its shell still holds the scope, so it is the scope's exact peak however short the run (until mq-s1, 2026-11-13, a run under 5 s recorded a shell's few MB); a scope gone by then, or a file it cannot read, leaves the sampler's last value. Its real peak, and both of a bounded unit's numbers, stay sampled. Reservations (`table`, the qualifying rule and the rise rule below) read only the real peak; `reclassify` reads neither, and the stall guard reads the live cgroup. Each heavy command reserves its class's GiB, after its locks: the largest real peak of the class's qualifying runs (ok, at least 60 s) plus 10%, rounded up to 0.5 GiB, once there are 3, and before that the seed in `$S/memory-reservations.tsv`, with its source; any recorded run above that raises it at once (`$S/admission.sh table` shows each basis). Light and full preps are separate classes; a light prep that turns full grows its reservation to the full prep's before its tests (`$S/admission.sh escalate`, ahead of every queued command) and is recorded as full. `capped.sh` commands reserve by what they run: `curator-typecheck` (the type check, one checker), `curator-lintlegs` (the full lint and its legs), `curator-lintpaths` (`lint:paths`), `curator-lint` (other lint forms: [operations](../orchestration/operations.md#gate-memory), "Which check reserves what"), `curator-corpus` (full-corpus `catalog:preview`/`catalog:curate`, `catalog:notice-deps`, which reads the whole catalog (5.26 GiB real), and whole-suite, directory or `--exclude` test runs), `curator-test` (named test files only; `curator-test-known`, 3 GiB, for a test set that has proved small, below), `curator-light` (`catalog:pin`, `catalog:format` and the other small package scripts) and `curator-inventory` (`catalog:launch-inventory` with `--write`, `--check`, `--verify --write` or `--verify --check`, and `mvp:status`, plus the measured small commands: `test:workflow`, `test:affected --base <rev>`, three catalog scripts with `--check` or `--write`, `pnpm install --offline`, the orchestration tests, and `node --import tsx scripts/catalog-record-review.ts` with `--list` (with or without `--json`) or `--verify` (with or without `--root <dir>`); through `pnpm -s`, also after `env -u`; any other form keeps its class: [operations](../orchestration/operations.md#gate-memory)); an unknown or composite command (`bash -c`, a script) is `curator`, never less than the largest kind, unless `CAPPED_KIND=lint|corpus|test|light` names its kind. `$S/admission.sh class capped 1 <command...>` shows a command's class, and `$S/admission.sh reclassify [--write]` relabels peaks.log lines recorded before their class was known (drain `capped.sh` first; it keeps a backup and leaves the log unchanged on any failure). A `capped.sh` line whose kind was declared says so: its command reads `<dir>: CAPPED_KIND=<kind> <command>`. `reclassify --declared <kind>` relabels those lines by their command's words, plus any ids it is given. It changes no other line, not even a plain `curator` or prep line. An unknown kind is refused. Lines from before this change don't record a declared kind, so relabel them by naming their ids. It is admitted only while the active reservations plus its own fit 18 GiB; otherwise it queues, gates and preps ahead of `capped.sh` commands (a `capped.sh` command that fits may start past an earlier one that does not, until the commands past it alone keep that one out; from then on nothing more starts ahead of it until it starts or is cancelled), and a gate or prep registers its intent before it waits for its locks, so no curator command starts ahead of it meanwhile. The exceptions avoid deadlock: commands whose job holds a lock the intent waits for (a bounded role's worker, which inherits `BOUNDED_HELD_LOCKS`) pass it and the commands queued behind it, and an intent waiting for a curator lock holds back no curator command. A `capped.sh` inside a bounded unit runs inline under the unit's reservation. Whether a gate overlaps a curator command depends on the recorded numbers. A reservation ends with its job's last process, however the job ends.

A `capped.sh` command stuck at its soft limit (at every sample: `memory.current` within 256 MiB of `memory.high`, the `high` counter rising, full memory pressure (avg300) at least 2.00%) is watched in two stages. After 300 s it is a stall suspect and runs on: a `stall-suspect` line in events.log, one on stderr and `stalled?` in `admission.sh status`, repeated every further 300 s. After 1,800 s the guard kills its process group, records the run as `stalled` (counted as `failed`), prints one line naming the limit, the time and the advice, and exits 79. One sample that fails any part, or a file that cannot be read, restarts both times, and a job that was a suspect gets a `stall-observed` line when it ends, so the stop time can be shortened from data ([stall guard](../orchestration/operations.md#gate-memory)). So run a type check as its own capped command, never inside a combined script: only a command whose own words name a type check gets the 16 GiB limit.

A `capped.sh` command that is a pure named-test run also adds a line to its test set's history, `$EP_ORCH/admission/test-sets/<sha256 of its key>`. The key is `workers=<n>` plus its test files, without `./`, deduplicated and sorted; the launcher does not count. It is evidence that exactly these files ran, all of their tests, so when in doubt there is none: each file must be an existing regular test file under `vitest.config.ts`'s include, run from the repository's root, that Vitest's substring filter matches alone. A `VAR=value` word, an `env -u` of a setting that decides the workers or what runs (`VITEST_*`, `BOUNDED_WORKERS`, `CI`), a declared kind, a `run` after the test script's own, a filter or glob, an option outside a short allow-list (`--reporter` only with a built-in reporter's name) and an allowed option with no proper value give no key. Each line is `<time> <id> <result> <seconds> <real peak> <memory.peak>`, appended under the admission mutex; `-t` runs are left out, and a store that cannot be written is skipped silently. `$S/admission.sh sets <command...>` shows a command's key and history, and `$S/admission.sh sets` prints one summary line; a history file whose first line isn't its key is reported and never counted (mq-s2).

A run with a key whose history has at least two ok runs, every recorded `memory.peak` at or under 2.7 GiB (whatever the run's result) and no run that outgrew the class reserves `curator-test-known`, 3 GiB, whatever class its words give it, `env -u ... pnpm -s test <file>` included; a `-t` run, a run with no key and a history file whose first line isn't its key reserve as before. The class reads one history file, outside the admission mutex, and never `peaks.log`; that reservation never goes below its seed, since 2.7 GiB is a settled threshold conservatively below 3 / 1.1. Such a run is also watched every 0.5 s: once its real use passes 2.7 GiB it is recorded as `curator-test` in `peaks.log` and as `outgrown-<result>` in its set's history (so the set no longer qualifies), and its reservation grows, at once when that fits, else ahead of every queued entry until the active jobs leave room. The size is never stored: every step that could grant the grow works it out again under the admission mutex, from the state of that moment, as the largest of `curator-test`'s seed (what the run would reserve without the small class; a run that outgrew it is no small test, so slow small runs that lowered `curator-test` say nothing about it), `curator-test`'s reservation, the run's own and its largest real use so far plus 10%, rounded up to 0.5 GiB; the grow field `status` shows is display only. So a grow never shrinks a reservation and always covers the run's use, meaning that when it is granted the run holds at least its largest real use seen by then (sampled every 0.5 s) plus 10%, rounded up, a rise while the grow was pending included; if its use plus 10% later rises past what it holds, it grows again by the same rule. The command is never stopped, delayed or limited for it: its scope keeps its 8 GiB soft and 12 GiB hard limits and its stall guard. At most one such grow is pending at a time; another outgrower waits for it. While a grow is pending or waiting, nothing queued starts, gates and preps included; that clears when the grow is granted or the command ends. There is no grow timeout, so a hung outgrower holds the queue until the stall guard stops it or it ends. `status` shows `growing to curator-test <n>G` and `outgrown: its grow to curator-test <n>G waits for another's`. Until its grow fits, a run uses more than it reserved, bounded only by its scope's limits: the slack is the slice's 20 GiB over the 18 GiB budget, and past 20 GiB the kernel's OOM killer picks a process in the slice, possibly another command's or a gate's (mq-s3; [operations](../orchestration/operations.md#gate-memory)).

A SIGKILLed `capped.sh` leaves its entry until the next sweep (any admission or `status`), which removes it once none of its processes is left; if it was already running, its command (a job of its own) keeps running, and keeps its reservation, until it ends.
