# Memory admission (#759)

Reference for [Chaining heavy jobs](../orchestration/runbook.md#chaining-heavy-jobs): how `admission.sh`, `bounded.sh` and `capped.sh` reserve memory. Read it when a job waits longer than expected or a class looks wrong; the operator actions are in the runbook.

A model session reserves no memory; its heavy commands do. Memory means `real`: memory.current − file + shmem, without reclaimable cache. Every bounded unit and `$EP_ORCH/capped.sh` scope samples its own real memory every 5 s; a unit's real peak goes into its receipt (`realPeakBytes`), and every run's goes into `$EP_ORCH/admission/peaks.log`. Each heavy command reserves its class's GiB, after its locks: the largest real peak of the class's qualifying runs (ok, at least 60 s) plus 10%, rounded up to 0.5 GiB, once there are 3, and before that the seed in `$S/memory-reservations.tsv`, with its source; any recorded run above that raises it at once (`$S/admission.sh table` shows each basis). Light and full preps are separate classes; a light prep that turns full grows its reservation to the full prep's before its tests (`$S/admission.sh escalate`, ahead of every queued command) and is recorded as full. `capped.sh` commands reserve by what they run: `curator-lint` (lint, typecheck), `curator-corpus` (full-corpus `catalog:preview`/`catalog:curate`, `catalog:notice-deps`, which reads the whole catalog (5.26 GiB real), and whole-suite, directory or `--exclude` test runs), `curator-test` (named test files only), `curator-light` (`catalog:pin`, `catalog:format` and the other small package scripts) and `curator-inventory` (`catalog:launch-inventory` with `--write`, `--check`, `--verify --write` or `--verify --check`, and `mvp:status`, through `pnpm -s`, also after `env -u`; any other form keeps its class); an unknown or composite command (`bash -c`, a script) is `curator`, never less than the largest kind, unless `CAPPED_KIND=lint|corpus|test|light` names its kind. `$S/admission.sh class capped 1 <command...>` shows a command's class, and `$S/admission.sh reclassify [--write]` relabels peaks.log lines recorded before their class was known (drain `capped.sh` first; it keeps a backup and leaves the log unchanged on any failure). A `capped.sh` line whose kind was declared says so: its command reads `<dir>: CAPPED_KIND=<kind> <command>`. `reclassify --declared <kind>` relabels those lines by their command's words, plus any ids it is given. It changes no other line, not even a plain `curator` or prep line. An unknown kind is refused. Lines from before this change don't record a declared kind, so relabel them by naming their ids. It is admitted only while the active reservations plus its own fit 18 GiB; otherwise it queues, gates and preps ahead of `capped.sh` commands, and a gate or prep registers its intent before it waits for its locks, so no curator command starts ahead of it meanwhile. The exceptions avoid deadlock: commands whose job holds a lock the intent waits for (a bounded role's worker, which inherits `BOUNDED_HELD_LOCKS`) pass it, and an intent waiting for a curator lock holds back no curator command. A `capped.sh` inside a bounded unit runs inline under the unit's reservation. Whether a gate overlaps a curator command depends on the recorded numbers. A reservation ends with its job's last process, however the job ends.

A `capped.sh` command stuck at its soft limit (`memory.current` within 256 MiB of `memory.high` with the `high` counter rising at every sample, full memory pressure (avg300) at least 2.00%) is watched in two stages. After 300 s it is a stall suspect and runs on: a `stall-suspect` line in events.log, one on stderr and `stalled?` in `admission.sh status`, repeated every further 300 s. After 1,800 s the guard kills its process group, records the run as `stalled` (counted as `failed`), prints one line naming the limit, the time and the advice, and exits 79. One sample off the limit restarts both times, and a job that was a suspect gets a `stall-observed` line when it ends, so the stop time can be shortened from data ([stall guard](../orchestration/operations.md#gate-memory)). So run a type check as its own capped command, never inside a combined script: only a command whose own words name a type check gets the 16 GiB limit.

A SIGKILLed `capped.sh` leaves its entry until the next sweep (any admission or `status`), which removes it once none of its processes is left; if it was already running, its command (a job of its own) keeps running, and keeps its reservation, until it ends.
